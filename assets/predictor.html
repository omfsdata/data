The interview-conversion estimator

Curious how your stats stack up? This tool takes four inputs — CBSE, class rank, externships, and research — and returns a predicted interview conversion rate. Drag the sliders and watch it update.

First, what “interview conversion” actually means: it’s the share of programs you apply to that send you an interview invite — invites ÷ applications. If you apply to 30 programs and 15 invite you, that’s 50%. It is not your chance of matching, and it’s not how many interviews you’ll get in total (that depends on how many places you apply). It’s a per-application hit rate — and it’s the stage where most of the sorting in this whole process happens.

How to read this — please open before trusting the number

This number is easy to misread, so here’s the honest fine print:

  • This predicts interviews, not matching. It cannot tell you your odds of matching — the underlying data contains only people who matched, so the model has never seen a non-matcher. A low estimate here is not a verdict on your candidacy, and a high one is not a guarantee.
  • It’s built entirely from success stories. The model was fit to 73 applicants who matched and chose to post their stats. That makes it a description of what conversion looked like among matchers, not a law that governs everyone. Every bias in who posts on a forum is baked in here too.
  • It gets shakier at the low end. Because matchers cluster toward the stronger end of the pool, the model has little data on low scores — so for a CBSE much below the mid-60s it’s extrapolating, and probably reading optimistically. Treat estimates down there as a loose ceiling, not a floor.
  • The real uncertainty is wide. Two applicants with identical stats can land far apart. The range shown is illustrative — read the output as “somewhere in this neighborhood,” never a precise figure.

Think of this as a way to feel how the model weighs each factor — nudge the CBSE slider and watch it move more than the others — rather than a forecast of your cycle. It’s a mirror of past forum data, not a crystal ball.
